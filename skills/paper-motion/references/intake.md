# Intake: learn everything before making anything

Most failed pieces fail before the first frame: the agent guessed. Motion has
too many dimensions (pace, transitions, camera, type, texture, sound, emotion)
to infer from one sentence or a few still images. So the intake is the longest
part of the job. Building starts only after the brief is written down and
approved.

## Rules

1. **Rounds, not a wall.** Ask 4–6 questions per round in the user's language,
   numbered so they can answer "3: …". Use multiple-choice when the user may
   lack the vocabulary, and open questions when you need their words. Run 3–4
   rounds. Stop early only when every field of the brief is filled from their
   answers, not from your assumptions.
2. **Motion needs motion references.** Still images say nothing about timing,
   transitions or camera. Always ask for 2–5 links to videos they love
   (Instagram, TikTok, Vimeo, YouTube, Behance, studio sites). For every
   reference, ask what exactly they like: pace, cuts, how the type moves,
   camera, texture, sound, mood. If they have none, show options by name and
   description (see "Vocabulary") and ask them to point.
3. **Anti-references count as much as references.** Ask what they hate, what
   looks "cheap", "AI" or "template" to them, and which previous attempts
   failed and why. Write it all down.
4. **Mirror back.** After each round, summarize in 3–5 lines what you now
   understand and what is still open. Ask them to correct you.
5. **The brief is a contract.** Write `brief.md` from the template below, show
   it, and get an explicit "sí / yes / approved". Do not build before that.
6. **Gates after the brief, in order:**
   - **A. Style frames:** 2–3 stills of key moments at final quality.
   - **B. Test shot:** 3–8 s of the most important moment at final quality,
     with motion blur.
   - **C. Full piece.**
   - **D. Polish.**

   Each gate needs approval. When a gate is rejected, go back to the intake
   questions it reveals ("what exactly felt wrong: pace, type, movement,
   composition, sound?") instead of guessing a new direction.
7. **Never show the same thing twice.** If something was rejected, ask what
   was wrong before changing it.

## Question bank

Pick from these per round. Skip what is already known; say what you already
know and ask them to confirm.

### Round 1 · purpose and references (always first)
- What is it for? Where will it be published, who watches it, and what should
  happen after they watch it?
- If the piece were a feeling, which 3 words describe it?
- Motion references: 2–5 links. For each one, what exactly do you like?
- Still references (images, posters, sites): what do you take from each? Name
  the concrete things: layout, type, color, an effect.
- What do you never want to see? What looks cheap, AI or template to you?
- Is there a deadline, or a date it has to come out for?

### Round 2 · content and structure
- Exact text: all of it, part of it, or none? Must it be read, or is it texture?
- Duration and format (9:16, 4:5, 1:1, 16:9). Loop or not.
- Pace: fast cuts (a change every 0.3–1 s), medium (1–3 s), or long shots
  (4 s+)? Sync it to music, a voice-over, or nothing?
- Structure: one continuous shot or camera move, a sequence of cuts, or
  chapters? How should it start and end?
- Hero moment: if only 2 seconds were remembered, which ones?

### Round 3 · look
- Color: exact palette or constraints (pure B&W, a brand color…). Background.
- Type: family or style (grotesk, mono, serif, display, handwritten). Show a
  specimen of 4–7 options on the real background and let them choose.
- Composition: big type filling the frame vs. small and precise; centered vs.
  grid or asymmetric; lots of negative space vs. dense.
- Texture: flat and clean, grain, print or halftone, 1-bit, glow…
- 2D, 3D, or both? Is there a camera?

### Round 4 · motion, sound, delivery
- How things move: snappy and sharp, smooth and floating, mechanical,
  physical (gravity, springs), glitchy…
- Transitions: hard cuts, match cuts, wipes, morphs, camera moves, none.
- Sound: music track (theirs?), synthesized effects (which feel: low,
  percussive, ambient…), voice-over, or silent.
- Delivery: formats, resolution, safe areas (UI overlays on reels), subtitles,
  length cut-downs.
- How will you judge it done? What would make you say "this is it"?

## Vocabulary (offer when the user has no references)

Describe each in one line so they can point at it:
- **Swiss kinetic type:** big grotesk type on a strict grid, hard cuts on the beat, flat colors.
- **Editorial minimal:** small precise type, lots of white space, slow camera, crisp fades.
- **Data / diagram:** lines, labels, nodes, charts drawn on, an instrument-like feel.
- **1-bit / dither / print:** pure black-and-white pixels, halftone, risograph, zine.
- **Cinematic 3D:** depth, light, a camera moving through a scene, shallow focus.
- **Liquid / organic:** fluid gradients, morphing shapes, soft and sensual.
- **Glitch / digital:** RGB split, datamosh, scanlines, noise, broken signal.
- **Collage / analog:** cutouts, paper, stop-motion jitter, tape.
- **Brutalist / poster:** huge type, raw layouts, aggressive cuts.

Studios and makers whose names work as shared vocabulary (ask the user to look
them up, never copy their work): DIA, ManvsMachine, Buck, Studio Dumbar,
Pentagram, Ordinary Folk, Tendril, Jeremiah Shaw, Animade.

## Brief template (`brief.md`)

```markdown
# Brief — <title>
Status: draft | approved by <user> on <date>

## Purpose
Where it lives · who watches · what should happen after watching.

## Feeling
Three words. One sentence of intent.

## References
- <link> — what we take from it: …
- <image> — what we take from it: …

## Anti-references (never)
- …

## Content
Exact text / none · must be read? · hero moment.

## Format
Aspect · duration · fps · loop · deliverables · safe areas.

## Structure and pace
Continuous / cuts / chapters · pace (cut every N s) · sync (music/VO/none) · opening · ending.

## Look
Palette · background · type (family, weights, sizes) · composition · texture · 2D/3D.

## Motion
Movement character · easing · transitions · camera · motion blur.

## Sound
Music / sfx (character) / VO / silent.

## Done when
The user's own words.

## Open questions
- …
```
